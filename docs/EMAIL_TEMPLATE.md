## Key Positioning Points

**What to emphasize:**
- System is complete and production-ready (not a proposal)
- Free pilot = zero financial risk
- They decide operations (we don't tell them how to work)
- USB scanners for warehouse, mobile for field techs
- Your success metric: They delay Business Central

**What NOT to say:**
- "This replaces PowerApps" (they never had it)
- "This is how you should run your operations" (too prescriptive)
- Heavy financial comparisons (you covered this already)
- Detailed technical architecture (unless they ask)

**If they ask about Business Central:**
"This system stands alone. It doesn't require Business Central.

If you already have NAV, we can extract data from there for the pilot. After that, you decide whether Business Central is still necessary for your inventory operations."

---

## Success Indicators

**You win if:**
- They agree to pilot
- They delay or cancel Business Central plans after seeing your system
- They realize manual work can skip straight to modern system

**Red flags:**
- "We're committed to Microsoft stack"
- "Need to wait for budget cycle" (push pilot now, payment later)
- Radio silence after demo (follow up persistently)

---

## Objection Handling

**"We don't have time for a pilot"**
"The pilot is designed around your operations. Stock count happens anyway. We're just using it to baseline the system. The only extra time is the 2-hour training."

**"What if it doesn't work?"**
"That's the point of the pilot. You test it with real work. If it doesn't work, you owe nothing and go back to manual processes."

**"We need IT to evaluate"**
"Absolutely. Bring your IT to the demo. Technical questions welcome."

**"Too expensive"**
"R1,680/month if you keep it. That's all. No per-user fees, no license renewals, no Microsoft lock-in."

**"We already committed to Business Central"**
"This is different. Business Central is full ERP. This is purpose-built for inventory operations. They can coexist, or you might find you don't need Business Central after all."
